# Phase 3 — Systemization (Repeatables, Not SOPs)

Goal: define how this venture operates as four repeatables — lightweight,
concrete workflows the founder runs weekly. Repeatables are not SOPs: an SOP
is a formalized procedure for someone else to execute; a repeatable is the
founder's own loop, kept cheap to change while the business is still finding
its shape. Each repeatable graduates to an SOP only when its scaling trigger
fires.

Produce one filled `templates/repeatable-one-pager.md` per repeatable,
customized to this venture's actual niche, tools, and constraints from
intake — generic workflows are a failed deliverable.

## The four repeatables

For each, specify: purpose, trigger (what starts a run), concrete steps with
named tools, where AI executes vs where human judgment is required (explicit
checkpoints), inputs/outputs, and a time budget that fits the founder's
stated hours per week.

### 1. How you research

The Phase 1 loop, made recurring: monitor the same source list on a cadence
for new complaints, competitor moves, and language shifts.

- AI executes: query runs, summarization, categorization into the Phase 1
  signal buckets, drafting the weekly signal digest.
- Human judgment checkpoint: deciding which signals change the offer or
  roadmap. AI proposes; founder disposes.

### 2. How you validate

Every material change (price, feature, segment, channel) gets a scaled-down
Phase 2 test before it is adopted: a threshold written in advance, a cheap
instrument (poll, landing page variant, five customer emails), a dated
verdict.

- AI executes: test design, copy variants, threshold math, results tally.
- Human judgment checkpoint: signing the threshold before the test runs, and
  accepting the verdict after — especially the kill verdicts.

### 3. How you create

The build/content loop: what gets made each week (product increment, content
pieces, sales assets), in what order, with what tools.

- AI executes: drafts, code scaffolding, repurposing one asset into many
  formats, editing passes.
- Human judgment checkpoint: what ships. Nothing customer-facing goes out
  without founder review; the founder's taste is the quality bar while the
  product finds its voice.

### 4. How you sell

The demand-capture loop: outreach, follow-up, waitlist nurture, closing
conversations — founder-led at this stage by design.

- AI executes: prospect list building from Phase 1 communities, first-draft
  outreach personalization, CRM hygiene, follow-up scheduling.
- Human judgment checkpoint: every actual customer conversation, pricing
  exceptions, and anything sent to a named human — AI drafts, founder sends.

## Minimum viable operating cadence

Prescribe a concrete weekly rhythm sized to the founder's stated hours.
Default shape for a ~10 hr/week solo founder (adjust to intake):

| Day | Block | Repeatable |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 1–2 h | Research: run digest, pick the week's one validation question |
| Tue–Thu | 4–6 h | Create: ship the week's increment |
| Fri | 1 h | Sell: outreach + follow-ups |
| Fri | 30 min | Metrics review + next-week decision (see dashboard) |

One validation question per week, maximum. More is thrash at this stage.

## Metrics dashboard spec — leading indicators only

At validation stage, track only what predicts revenue, not revenue-stage
vanity metrics. One screen, updated in the Friday review. Suggested spec
(tool: a spreadsheet or Notion table; no BI tooling yet):

| Metric | Why leading |
|---|---|
| New demand signals logged this week | Research repeatable is alive |
| Validation tests run / verdicts reached | Learning velocity |
| Customer conversations held | Precedes every early sale |
| Outreach sent → replies | Sell-loop conversion, top of funnel |
| Waitlist / email list net adds | Stored demand |
| Content pieces shipped → meaningful responses | Build-in-public compounding |

Explicitly exclude at this stage: revenue projections, MRR charts, follower
counts, page views without capture. Revenue itself is recorded when it
happens (it is the point) but is not a dashboard *target* until Phase 4's
first-10-customers plan is underway.

## Scaling triggers — when a repeatable becomes an SOP

A repeatable graduates to a formal, documented SOP when **any** of:

1. It has been executed ~10+ times with a stable step sequence (steps no
   longer change run to run).
2. Someone other than the founder needs to run it (hire, contractor, or
   co-founder handoff).
3. Run-to-run time variance is the bottleneck — the loop is reliable but
   slow, and standardization would recover hours.
4. An error in the loop has cost real money or a customer, and a checklist
   would have prevented it.

Until a trigger fires, resist documentation overhead: the one-pager is the
whole system. Record each repeatable's trigger status in the Phase 3
deliverable so the founder knows what graduation will look like.

## Deliverable

Four filled one-pagers (`templates/repeatable-one-pager.md`), the weekly
cadence table, the dashboard spec, and a scaling-trigger status line per
repeatable.
